# Legal, Permit, and Practical Constraints on Geospatial and Street-Level Data Collection in Lebanon (Sidewalk Mapping)

> These are research notes, not legal advice. Today is 2026-11-06. Lebanon has been in an active conflict for much of 2026, so rules and enforcement are changing quickly. Most English-language sources on drones and photography are travel blogs and aggregators, not primary legal texts. Each claim carries its source, and weaker sources are flagged.

## 1. Drones: legal status, approving bodies, process, 2023-2026 changes, import

### Takeaway
There is no dedicated Lebanese drone law. In practice the Lebanese Army Command controls permits for camera drones. On **1 March 2026 the Army suspended all drone flight permits nationwide "until further notice"** amid regional escalation, and I found no report that the suspension has been lifted. A drone survey of Beirut sidewalks should be treated as **not currently feasible** unless the team gets written Army authorisation directly.

### Cited Findings
- **Nationwide suspension (most important finding):** LBCI reported on 1 March 2026 that "the Lebanese army has suspended all drone flight permits issued by its command across the country," effective 1 March 2026 "until further notice." The stated reason was "regional developments" and the army's "ongoing efforts to monitor and maintain security nationwide." The article quotes no formal statement and lists no penalties. — [LBCI, 1 Mar 2026](https://v7cdn.lbcgroup.tv/news/lebanon-news/908645/lebanese-army-suspends-drone-permits-nationwide-amid-regional-tensions/en)
- **The Army, not municipalities, is the permit authority:** criminal law specialist Itaf Qamar Al-Din says "The authority to issue permits does not lie with the municipality, but rather with the Lebanese Army." She describes a written application with supporting documents for small "flying cam" drones. She also warns that the Army's permit power has no clear rules or standards and could be abused. — [SMEX, 8 Oct 2025](https://smex.org/drones-in-lebanon-who-can-fly-them-and-are-they-legal/)
- **No specific drone legislation:** SMEX says Lebanon lacks clear drone legislation and calls for a specific privacy law. It contrasts Lebanon with Egypt's Law 216/2017, which makes unauthorised drone import or possession punishable by 1-7 years in prison. — [SMEX, Oct 2025](https://smex.org/drones-in-lebanon-who-can-fly-them-and-are-they-legal/)
- **Municipalities have tried to regulate drones anyway:** Jezzine Municipal Decision No. 883 requires prior written permission to use drones and the handover of drone-captured media. SMEX's expert says municipalities have "no such authority" under Legislative Decree 118/1977 (Municipal Law), Art. 47, and that such decisions can be challenged before the State Shura Council. Fanar municipality runs its own drone monitoring. — [SMEX, Oct 2025](https://smex.org/drones-in-lebanon-who-can-fly-them-and-are-they-legal/)
- **Law 81/2018 can be used against drone imagery:** SMEX notes that the law covers collecting, processing, and disclosing personal data, including identifying photos, and can be used for prosecution. — [SMEX, Oct 2025](https://smex.org/drones-in-lebanon-who-can-fly-them-and-are-they-legal/)
- **Pre-2026 process (secondary sources, weak):**
  - Several travel guides say the Army requires companies and individuals flying camera drones to get a special permit before flying. One lists email, fax, or in-person submission to Army Command, with no fee. — [drone-laws.com (2026 page, largely unsourced)](https://drone-laws.com/drone-laws-in-lebanon/)
  - One guide says requests go to the Air Force and take at least a week, often longer. — [onmetravel (cited via search snippet; page had an expired certificate)](https://onmetravel.com/taking-photos-in-lebanon)
  - One guide says the Army asks for a passport copy with the Lebanese entry stamp, so visitors often cannot apply before they arrive. — [fitcationhq](https://fitcationhq.com/lebanon-drone-regulations-for-tourists-2026-guide/)
  - An older 961 headline reports that special permits are now required. The article body could not be retrieved and it is several years old. — [The961](https://www.the961.com/special-permit-needed-to-fly-drones-lebanon/)
  - Forum posts say flying over cities or where the army is visible is prohibited. — [Tripadvisor forum (anecdotal)](https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowTopic-g294004-i2871-k14335279-Drone_laws_in_Lebanon-Lebanon.html)
- **Conflicting claims about which body approves:**
  - Backpack.travel says every drone must be registered with civil aviation, each flight needs its own permit via the Army, and some DJI models are refused. It also lists Lebanon as a country where drones are "banned." — [Backpack.travel](https://backpack.travel/en/drone-ranking/)
  - Other guides name the DGCA (Ministry of Public Works and Transport) as the prior-approval body, or the Ministry of Telecommunications. — [UAV Coach](https://uavcoach.com/drone-laws-in-lebanon/); [drone-traveller.com](https://drone-traveller.com/drone-laws-lebanon/)
  - These conflicts are unresolved. The SMEX legal expert and the LBCI report both point to the Army as the operative authority.
- **Import and customs:**
  - Travel guides say a drone without a permit may be confiscated at customs. — [UAV Coach](https://uavcoach.com/drone-laws-in-lebanon/); [drone-traveller.com](https://drone-traveller.com/drone-laws-lebanon/)
  - Lebanese security agencies seized drones and quadcopters at Tripoli port, reported in 2026 (Arabic). — [Annahar](https://www.annahar.com/lebanon/335825/الجمارك-اللبنانية-تضبط-درونات-ودراجات-رباعية-مقصوصة-ومعاد-تلحيمها-في-طرابلس)
  - LBCI reported a suspect detained over alleged drone-equipment imports. — [LBCI](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/lebanon-news/941199/lbci-sources-suspect-detained-over-alleged-drone-equipment-imports-for/en)
  - The New Arab (June 2026) reported that no decision bans dual-use fibre-optic cables used in FPV drones. — [The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/exclusive-dual-use-materials-hezbollah-fpvs-cant-be-banned)
- **Airspace context:** several European regulators advise against flying through Lebanese airspace because of military activity linked to the Israel-Hezbollah conflict. — [SafeAirspace](https://safeairspace.net/lebanon/)

### Inferences
- Given the March 2026 suspension, Israeli drone activity over Beirut, and the security services' sensitivity to drones (Hezbollah and Israeli FPV and surveillance drones), a civilian research drone over Beirut streets would risk confiscation, detention, or being mistaken for a hostile platform. Plan the project without drones, and use satellite or very-high-resolution commercial imagery for the aerial layer.
- If drones become essential later, the realistic route is an institutional request (a university letter, ideally through a Lebanese partner institution) to Army Command, after the suspension is lifted.

### Gaps
- No primary text found for any Army or DGCA regulation, application form, or official permit procedure, and no confirmed fee or timeline.
- Could not confirm whether the 1 March 2026 suspension has been lifted as of November 2026.
- Could not confirm the legal basis for customs confiscation, or whether import needs prior Army or Ministry approval.

## 2. Street photography and 360° imagery: restrictions, sensitive areas, letters, Street View

### Takeaway
Ground-level photography is legal in principle. The practical risks come from photographing military or security sites and checkpoints, and from Hezbollah-controlled areas, especially Dahiyeh, where media workers are informally expected to get Hezbollah permission and have been detained or had footage deleted. Google has never driven Street View cars in Lebanon. It has only a 2018 "special collection" of 41 landmarks captured by backpack.

### Cited Findings
- **Hezbollah areas:**
  - Reporting from 2021 states that journalists and media workers must ask Hezbollah for permission before reporting from the southern suburbs. A British reporter, Matt Kynaston, was detained in Dahiyeh while covering the fuel crisis because he had not asked permission. — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/mena/lebanon/journalists-freed-after-being-detained-in-hezbollah-stronghold-1.1250746)
  - Earlier cases include Brazilian journalists and a French photographer detained in Dahiyeh in 2008. — [CPJ, 2008](https://cpj.org/2008/08/lebanon-tv-crew-harassed-by-hezbollah-how-cpj-inve/)
- **2025 obstruction:** SKeyes documented Hezbollah members and supporters preventing an MTV crew from filming in Burj al-Barajneh and deleting their footage, among other violations against media workers. — [SKeyes, 2025](https://www.skeyesmedia.org/en/News/Reports/26-02-2025/12321); [SKeyes, Apr 2025](https://www.skeyesmedia.org/en/News/Reports/06-04-2025/12432)
- **Long-standing photography warnings:** visitors have been told to beware of photographing sensitive sites. — [Emirates 24/7, 2015](https://www.emirates247.com/news/visitors-to-lebanon-beware-of-photographing-2015-05-06-1.589773)
- **Journalists killed in 2026:** Lebanese journalists were killed by Israeli strikes in March 2026, which illustrates the physical risk of camera work during the 2026 war. — [The National, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/03/30/three-lebanese-journalists-killed-in-israeli-strike-laid-to-rest)
- **Google Street View:**
  - Lebanon's coverage is limited to a "special collection" of 41 landmarks and culturally significant sites (Jeita Grotto, Baalbek, National Museum, etc.), launched in late 2018 and captured with the wearable Street View Trekker backpack. — [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/industries/technology/404221-google-street-view-launches-lebanon-collection); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts/google-brings-lebanon-s-cultural-sites-to-the-world-in-immersive-360-1.769452); [The961](https://www.the961.com/google-street-view-lebanons-landmarks)
  - Google said at launch that it hoped to add more of Lebanon. I found no report of road coverage since then. — [Wikipedia: Google Street View in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Google_Street_View_in_Asia)

### Inferences
- The absence of drive-by Street View is most plausibly linked to security sensitivities (an Army and security services culture of restricting imaging), but **Google has published no stated reason**. Do not attribute one.
- For a 360° or phone-camera sidewalk survey:
  - Carry a university letter and ID, plus a Lebanese partner's letter in Arabic.
  - Get a letter from the Beirut Governor or municipality if possible. Beirut's executive authority is the Governor, Marwan Abboud.
  - Avoid pointing cameras at Army or ISF posts, checkpoints, barracks, embassies, ministries, the Parliament area (Nejmeh Square cordon), and party offices.
  - Do not collect imagery in Dahiyeh or Palestinian camps (Shatila, Burj al-Barajneh, Mar Elias) without prior coordination with local gatekeepers: the municipality, Hezbollah media relations for Dahiyeh, and the camp popular committees.
  - Delete-on-demand requests should be expected and complied with.

### Gaps
- No primary rule found that requires a municipal permit for street photography. No written Army list of no-photo zones.
- No sourced information found on rules for photographing inside Palestinian camps (governed de facto by camp security committees). Not searched in depth.
- No source gives Google's reasons for not running Street View cars.

## 3. Access to official geospatial data (DAG, cadastre, CDR/DGU)

### Takeaway
The Army's Directorate of Geographic Affairs (DAG) produces the national base maps (1:20,000 unified map) and does paid work for public and private bodies. There is no published open-data or sales procedure. Cadastre sits with the Ministry of Finance's land registry and cadastre system, which was being modernised under a World Bank project. Expect formal written requests, and expect access to be slow and discretionary.

### Cited Findings
- **DAG mandate and products:**
  - The DAG is authorised to perform work for "governmental departments, municipalities and public or private institutions at the expense of the concerned parties."
  - It lists a unified 1:20,000 map of all Lebanese territory.
  - Address: Aaraya, Damascus Road.
  - [Lebanese Army DAG page](https://www.lebarmy.gov.lb/en/node/3984)
- **Commercial resale:** East View Geospatial resells DAG products in print, raster, and vector GIS formats. Some 1:50,000 sheets date from 1929-1966. — [geospatial.com country profile](https://geospatial.com/country_profiles/lebanon/)
- **UN listing:** the UN SALB listing for the DAG shows "No datasets currently available." — [UN SALB](https://salb.un.org/en/node/354)
- **Survey infrastructure:** in 2023 the DAG launched a 13-station online geodetic network (a CORS-type network for surveying), costing $700,000 and funded by USAID and the DAG budget. — [LBCI, 2023](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/lebanon-news/691386/lebanese-army-launches-online-network-to-facilitate-geographical-surve/en)
- **Cadastre:** the World Bank Land Administration System Modernization Project (2018) aims to improve access to geospatial and property data through the Land Registry and Cadastre System. — [World Bank, 8 Jun 2018](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/loans-credits/2018/06/08/lebanon-land-administration-system-modernization-project)
- **Historical context:** a FIG 2002 paper describes Lebanese surveying and cadastre institutions. — [FIG 2002](https://www.fig.net/resources/proceedings/fig_proceedings/fig_2002/Ts7-10/TS7_10_fares.pdf)

### Inferences
- For sidewalk mapping, open sources (OpenStreetMap, commercial very-high-resolution satellite imagery, Beirut municipality or UN-Habitat post-blast datasets) are likely more practical than DAG data. The DAG and cadastre are worth approaching only for base geometry (road and parcel lines), through an institutional request.

### Gaps
- No found information on DAG pricing, licensing terms, or whether foreigners can buy data.
- CDR (Council for Development and Reconstruction) and DGU (Directorate General of Urbanism) data-sharing practices were not found in this pass. The National Physical Master Plan (SDATL) shapefiles were not checked.
- Post-2020 port blast datasets (e.g., UN-Habitat or Beirut Urban Lab) were not checked here.

## 4. Data protection: Law No. 81/2018 and faces and licence plates

### Takeaway
Law 81/2018 (10 Oct 2018) covers both automated and non-automated processing of personal data, and identifiable photos fall within it. It has **no independent data protection authority**, and its implementing regulations were reportedly never issued. Enforcement is minimal, but the law can still ground prosecutions or complaints. Blurring faces and licence plates before storing or publishing imagery is the defensible practice.

### Cited Findings
- **Basics:** enacted 10 October 2018. It covers electronic transactions, personal data, and cybersecurity, and applies to automatic and non-automatic processing. — [DLA Piper Data Protection](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=LB&t=law); [dig.watch](https://dig.watch/resource/electronic-transactions-and-personal-data-law-law-no-81-of-lebanon)
- **Not comprehensive:** it is described as a combined e-transactions and data law, not a comprehensive data protection framework, with no general data protection authority. Some processing needs licences or declarations, reportedly through the Ministry of Economy. Data subjects have rights to information, access, rectification, and objection. — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=LB&t=law); [Council of Europe study (FR)](https://rm.coe.int/3692-1-2-etude-loi-pdp-liban-coe-108-/1680a1c981)
- **Criticism:** EuroMed Monitor says the law "fails to protect people's privacy," citing broad exemptions and executive control. — [EuroMed Monitor](https://euromedmonitor.org/en/article/5630/Lebanon:-Data-protection-law-fails-to-protect-people%E2%80%99s-privacy-and-data)
- **Implementing decrees:** a Byblos Bank report covers the government's details on the law. Other sources say the implementing regulations have not been issued. — [Byblos Bank Lebanon This Week](https://www.byblosbank.com/common/economic-research-new/lebanon-this-week/lebanon-this-week-561/government-issues-details-of-law-on-electronic-transactions-and-personal-data)
- **Applies to identifying photos:** SMEX's legal expert says Law 81/2018 covers the collection, processing, and disclosure of personal data including identifying photos, and can be used for prosecution. She also notes that Lebanese criminal law lacks an equivalent of French Penal Code Art. 226-1 on photographing people in private spaces. — [SMEX, Oct 2025](https://smex.org/drones-in-lebanon-who-can-fly-them-and-are-they-legal/)

### Inferences
- Minimal practice for a sidewalk dataset:
  - Run automated face and licence-plate blurring at ingestion, as Mapillary and KartaView already do.
  - Store raw imagery encrypted, with restricted access and a defined retention period.
  - Publish only blurred imagery and derived features (sidewalk widths, obstruction points).
  - Avoid capturing into private spaces such as windows and courtyards.
  - Avoid publishing imagery that reveals security installations.
- Because the regulator is weak, the funder's and the university's requirements (GDPR for EU partners, IRB conditions) will in practice be the binding standard.

### Gaps
- Could not get an English text of the relevant articles on consent, sensitive data, or the licence and declaration regime. Whether filming public streets for research needs a declaration to the Ministry of Economy is unconfirmed.

## 5. University ethics and IRB in Lebanon

### Takeaway
AUB's IRB requires review of all human-subjects research done by AUB/AUBMC personnel or at AUB, wherever the research happens, unless it is formally exempted. Whether pure observation of sidewalks and objects (with incidental capture of people) is exempt is decided by the IRB, so teams should request an exemption determination rather than assume one.

### Cited Findings
- **AUB policy:** all research involving human subjects requires IRB review and approval unless exempted. The IRB reviews research conducted at or by AUB/AUBMC regardless of funding source or location. — [AUB IRB About Us](https://www.aub.edu.lb/irb/Pages/Aboutus.aspx); [AUB IRB announcements](https://www.aub.edu.lb/irb/Pages/announcements.aspx)
- **Comparative norms (non-Lebanese, for context):**
  - Unengaged observation of public behaviour where people have no expectation of privacy is typically exempt. — [UC Berkeley CPHS](https://cphs.berkeley.edu/guide/behavioral-observation.html)
  - Not all behaviour in public spaces counts as "public behaviour." — [UW-La Crosse tipsheet](https://www.uwlax.edu/globalassets/offices-services/grants/human-subjects-research/irb-tipsheet_public-vs.-private-spaces.docx)
  - IRBs may require disclosure measures (visible badges) when recording in public. — [UC Berkeley CPHS](https://cphs.berkeley.edu/guide/behavioral-observation.html)

### Inferences
- A sidewalk audit that measures infrastructure is arguably not human-subjects research. Interviews with shop owners, pedestrian intercept surveys, or disability-user walk-alongs clearly are, and will need full IRB review plus informed consent, likely in Arabic.
- Foreign teams will typically need their home IRB approval, and possibly local IRB approval if partnering with AUB, LAU, USJ, or BAU.

### Gaps
- No information found on LAU, USJ, or BAU IRB procedures, or on any national research-ethics body or government research permit for foreigners. No evidence of a general national "research permit" requirement was found.

## 6. Lebanese regulations defining sidewalks (benchmarks)

### Takeaway
The legal framework exists but is fragmented and weakly enforced:
- **Building law:** Law 646/2004 and Decree 15874/2005.
- **Traffic law:** Law 243/2012, which fines sidewalk parking.
- **Disability law:** Law 220/2000 and Decree 7194/2011, which set accessibility rules for public-use buildings.
- **Beirut rules on occupying the public domain:** actively enforced in November 2026 under Warning 11866/2026.

I could **not find a sourced statutory minimum sidewalk width**. This is a key gap for benchmarking.

### Cited Findings
- **Building Law:**
  - Law No. 646, dated 16 Dec 2004 (Official Gazette No. 66), with Enforcement Decree No. 15874 of Dec 2005. Sources disagree on the decree date (5 or 12 Dec 2005).
  - The building envelope is tied to road width plus setbacks (2×(road + setbacks)).
  - [Civil Society Knowledge Centre](https://civilsociety-centre.org/paper/lebanon-2004-construction-law-inside-parliamentary-debates)
  - The only dimension found in an unofficial copy of the decree concerns side passages (max 4 m), not sidewalks. — [Slideshare copy of the decree](https://www.slideshare.net/Eliemalti/lebanese-building-law)
- **Building violations and encroachment:** an overview of licensing and encroachment rules. — [lebaneselegislation.com](https://lebaneselegislation.com/en/topic/building-violations-encroachment/)
- **Traffic Law 243/2012:**
  - Passed in 2012, replacing a 1967 law, with phased enforcement from 2015.
  - A blog list of fines puts "parking on sidewalks" at 150,000 LBP versus 100,000 LBP for illegal parking. This is a secondary source; pre-crisis LBP amounts are now nearly worthless after the currency collapse, and any update is unverified.
  - [StateofMind13](https://stateofmind13.com/tag/fines/); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/world/lebanon-attempts-to-impose-order-on-its-traffic-jungle-1.133091)
- **Law 220/2000 (rights of persons with disabilities):**
  - Art. 37 requires public-use buildings to meet minimum accessibility standards, with the Ministry of Social Affairs proposing lists of buildings to upgrade.
  - Its accessibility provisions were largely not implemented, and the law set a six-year horizon for accessible environments.
  - [Executive Magazine](https://www.executive-magazine.com/business-all/real-estate/lebanon-disabled-buildings); [UPR submission](https://uprdoc.ohchr.org/uprweb/downloadfile.aspx?filename=8071&file=EnglishTranslation); [UK FCDO/DFID disability report, 2018](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5b584da340f0b633af812655/Disability_in_Lebanon.pdf)
- **Decree 7194/2011:** covers accessible parking, entrances, ramps, elevators, and toilets in **new public-use buildings** registered after its enforcement. No found source says it covers public sidewalks. — [Executive Magazine](https://www.executive-magazine.com/business-all/real-estate/lebanon-disabled-buildings); [HRW, 2018](https://www.hrw.org/report/2018/03/22/i-would-go-school/barriers-education-children-disabilities-lebanon)
- **Sidewalk accessibility standards:** L'Orient Today reported that the Lebanese Standards Institution (LIBNOR) was developing accessibility specifications for public roadways and sidewalks (date unclear; page returned 403). — [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1312987/sidewalk-struggles-beirut-streets-pose-challenge-to-pedestrians.html)
- **Barriers to visually impaired pedestrians in Beirut:** academic study. — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/33234392/Visually_impaired_pedestrians_between_mobility_and_urban_planning_the_case_of_Beirut)
- **Beirut public-domain occupation (very recent):** on 2 Nov 2026, Beirut Governor Marwan Abboud issued final general Warning No. 11866/2026 against irregular occupation of sidewalks, traffic lanes, and public domain. [Libnanews](https://en.libnanews.com/beirut-abboud-sets-violators-free-sidewalks-global-voices) is the source for every point in this list.
  - **What it targets:**
    - tables, chairs, tents, umbrellas, flower boxes, and metal barriers
    - goods, shelves, displays, trolleys, stalls, and coffee or beverage machines beyond the establishment's legal limits
    - extensions under balconies
    - private obstacles reserving road space
    - parking on sidewalks
    - valet companies reserving sidewalk or carriageway, or operating without a licence
  - **Enforcement:** field inspections begin **16 Nov 2026**, carried out by the Beirut Police Unit, municipal services, and the Municipal Guard Regiment.
  - **Sanctions:** reports, suspension of authorisations, and temporary closure with red seals.
  - **Licensing:** valid occupation authorisations remain recognised only within their stated area and period, and renewals are not automatic.
  - **Related:** Joint Decision No. 1508 of 15 Oct 2026 caps valet fees at 500,000 LBP.
  - **Not specified:** sidewalk dimensions or a minimum clear width.
- **Sidewalk occupation as a documented phenomenon:**
  - Beirut public space privatisation and sidewalk occupation by cafés, restaurants, valet parking, and similar uses. — [Nahnoo, "From Public to Private" (2025)](https://nahnoo.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/FROM-PUBLIC-TO-PRIVATE.pdf)
  - Pedestrian obstacles (parked cars, dumpsters, café seating). — [L'Orient Today](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1312987/sidewalk-struggles-beirut-streets-pose-challenge-to-pedestrians.html)
  - Street context. — [Hamra Street (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamra_Street)

### Inferences
- For a benchmark, the team could:
  - code obstructions against the categories in Warning 11866/2026, which is effectively a legal typology of sidewalk obstructions;
  - code against Law 243/2012 (parked vehicles on the sidewalk);
  - and, absent a Lebanese minimum width, use international benchmarks (e.g., a 1.2-1.5 m clear path under ADA/ISO 21542 norms), stating clearly that these are not Lebanese law.
- The 16 Nov 2026 enforcement campaign creates a natural before/after window: imagery captured before and after it could measure enforcement effects. Shop owners may also be more suspicious of anyone photographing their frontage during that period.

### Gaps
- No sourced minimum sidewalk width under Law 646/2004, Decree 15874/2005, the urban-planning law (Legislative Decree 69/1983), or the Beirut municipal regulations. The Arabic texts need checking on legiliban / the Official Gazette, or through the Order of Engineers and Architects.
- No text found for the Beirut municipality fee schedule or permit procedure for terrace or kiosk occupation (permits for "إشغال الأملاك العامة").
- The current LBP value of the sidewalk-parking fine is unverified.
- Whether LIBNOR ever published sidewalk accessibility standards is unconfirmed.

## 7. Security and practical fieldwork risks, 2024-2026

### Takeaway
Lebanon is under a US **Level 4 "Do Not Travel"** advisory, last updated about 23 Oct 2026. Beirut itself has been struck in the 2026 war, including central Beirut (Corniche al-Mazraa) and southern Beirut on 8 April 2026, with 357 killed nationwide that day. Strikes continued and were escalating as of late October 2026. Fieldwork in Dahiyeh and the south is high-risk. Even central Beirut needs a security plan, check-in protocols, and suspension triggers.

### Cited Findings
- **US advisory:**
  - Level 4 for all of Lebanon, citing crime, terrorism, unrest, kidnapping, landmines, and armed conflict.
  - Drone and missile strikes can occur throughout Lebanon, including Beirut.
  - Dahiyeh and southern Beirut, and southern Lebanon, are named specifically.
  - Embassy staff need permission for personal travel.
  - Kidnapping of foreigners has been warned of.
  - [US State Dept advisory](https://state.gov/lebanon-travel-advisory-remains-level-4-do-not-travel/); [US Embassy Beirut, Oct 2026](https://lb.usembassy.gov/travel-advisory-lebanon-october-2026/); [US Embassy, Aug 2026](https://lb.usembassy.gov/travel-advisory-lebanon-august-2026/)
- **8 April 2026 strikes:**
  - The 2026 Lebanon war was part of a wider 2026 Iran war.
  - The 8 April 2026 strikes hit central Beirut (Corniche al-Mazraa) without warning, and Hay el-Sellom in southern Beirut.
  - 357 killed and 1,223 injured nationwide that day.
  - Israel-Lebanon ceasefire talks were announced on 10 April 2026.
  - [Wikipedia: 8 April 2026 Israeli attacks on Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8_April_2026_Israeli_attacks_on_Lebanon)
- **Other Beirut strike:** an airstrike hit Ramlet al-Baida in Beirut in 2026. — [Wikipedia: 2026 Ramlet al-Baida airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Ramlet_al-Baida_airstrike)

### Inferences
- **Operational recommendations:**
  - Lebanese team members lead fieldwork.
  - Survey in daylight and in short sessions.
  - Avoid Dahiyeh, the south, and areas near party offices or recent strike sites.
  - Monitor Israeli evacuation warnings, which are posted on social media before many strikes.
  - Carry letters in Arabic.
  - Keep imagery off devices at checkpoints where possible (upload and wipe).
  - Do not photograph strike damage sites, which are highly sensitive to both Hezbollah and the Army.
  - Get institutional travel-risk and insurance clearance, since many universities bar travel under Level 4.
- **Power and connectivity:** state electricity is rationed and generators are common, so plan battery banks for 360° cameras and phones, and plan for intermittent mobile data for uploads. This is general knowledge, not sourced in this pass.
