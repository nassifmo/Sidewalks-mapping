# Measure Lebanon's Sidewalks From the Street Up

The 2019–2026 literature gives a clear answer. Overhead imagery is good at finding *where* sidewalks are. Only street-level imagery, checked against field measurements, can say *how wide* they are and *what is in the way*, and curb height still needs a tape or a phone LiDAR. The best open overhead pipeline, Tile2Net, reaches **82.7% sidewalk IoU on 15–20 cm US orthophotos**, but its overlap falls to 77.9% in a city it never saw in training, it ships no fine-tuning code, and nothing has been published on its performance outside the US. Beirut's free satellite imagery is 0.31–0.50 m and licensed for manual tracing only, so automatic overhead extraction cannot be the backbone for Lebanon. The strongest street-level width method (UrbanVGGT, 2026) reaches **0.25 m mean absolute error, but only when the camera height is known**; on ordinary phone photos the same approach degrades to about 0.7 m, and obstruction detectors lose a third or more of their recall when moved from US cities to a Mexican one. The ideal framework for Lebanon is therefore street-level first. It hangs eight modules on the per-side sidewalk segment ID that the team's Beirut pilot already defines: an OSM- and footprint-derived segment network, walking 360° capture at a fixed camera height, Mask2Former-plus-3D width measurement, a YOLO-class obstruction detector fine-tuned on the pilot's 21 Lebanese obstruction types, rule-based fusion with method-ranked provenance, stratified field validation, indicators coded to Beirut's November 2026 occupation-enforcement categories, and OpenSidewalks/OSM publication under ODbL. The pilot is currently a well-designed but empty schema. Its first deliverable should be a hand-measured ground-truth census of Mar Mikhael–Gemmayzeh, which calibrates every model that follows, and the one step that cannot wait is street capture before enforcement starts on 16 November 2026.

## Overhead finds sidewalks, street level measures them, and nothing measures curbs

### Overhead extraction works in the US at sub-30 cm and has not been shown to work anywhere else

Tile2Net is the only maintained end-to-end open pipeline from tiles to sidewalk polygons to a routable network. It is BSD-3-Clause, was last committed in September 2026, and needs a CUDA GPU ([GitHub](https://github.com/VIDA-NYU/tile2net)). Its HRNet-W48 + OCR hierarchical multi-scale attention model was trained on about 12,000 tiles from Cambridge, DC and New York, labelled from municipal planimetric GIS with 5,620 tiles hand-corrected. It reported **sidewalk IoU of 82.67% (precision 0.90, recall 0.92)** and area overlap of 85.9% in Cambridge, which was in training, against 77.9% in Boston, which was not ([Hosseini et al. 2023](https://fmiranda.me/publications/pdfs/tile2net.pdf)). The maintainers ask for imagery at "zoom 19 or higher if possible, and no less than 18" and say they "only support inference" ([BASICS.md](https://github.com/VIDA-NYU/tile2net/blob/main/BASICS.md)). A 2025 request from Brazilian users for a fine-tuning module was closed without code ([Issue #84](https://github.com/VIDA-NYU/tile2net/issues/84)), and Esri's commercial port of the model requires 10–30 cm RGB and is scoped to US states ([Esri](https://doc.arcgis.com/en/pretrained-models/latest/imagery/introduction-to-pedestrian-infrastructure-classification-model.htm)). At Beirut's latitude, Web-Mercator zoom 18 is about 0.50 m per pixel and zoom 19 about 0.25 m (a computed figure, not a sourced one). The current Esri imagery over Achrafieh is 0.50 m WorldView-2, so it sits at Tile2Net's floor before licensing is even considered.

The alternatives are weaker. GeoSAM fine-tunes SAM with Tile2Net prompts on a single RTX 4090 and reached a pedestrian-infrastructure IoU of only **0.45 in DC and 0.27 in unseen Cambridge**, against 0.12 for zero-shot SAM. Its authors note that SAM struggles "in distinguishing sidewalks from roads in aerial imagery" ([GeoSAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.11319v2)). SAMGeo (MIT licence) wraps SAM, SAM 2, SAM 3 and text prompting and exports GeoPackage. It is a labelling accelerator rather than a sidewalk detector, and no sidewalk accuracy has been published for it ([segment-geospatial](https://github.com/opengeos/segment-geospatial)). Geospatial foundation models such as Prithvi, Clay and SatlasPretrain are either pretrained at 10–30 m or have no sidewalk class, which makes them irrelevant at this scale ([SatlasPretrain](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.15660)).

Resolution is the binding constraint. At 0.3 m a sidewalk spans 4–5 pixels, and Ning et al. reached about 0.9 precision and recall only with a tolerance buffer and street-view gap-filling; strict pixel precision was about 0.55 ([Ning et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2399808321995817)). Domain shift compounds it: APE's sidewalk-graph F1 fell from 80.5 in Los Angeles to 74.6 in Quito, where the imagery was coarser ([APE](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.02323)). No 2019–2026 study tested any overhead sidewalk model in a Middle Eastern city.

### Topology is better built from the street graph than from skeletons

Turning masks into a network is the most fragile step. Tile2Net's dense-Voronoi centrelines are "very sensitive" to interpolation distance and break at polygon boundaries ([Hosseini et al. 2023](https://fmiranda.me/publications/pdfs/tile2net.pdf)). PathwayBench found Tile2Net's edge F1 respectable at 0.76–0.90 but its routability poor, with TraversabilitySimilarity of **0.04 in Portland and 0.17 in Seattle**. Prophet, a graph-first method that starts from the street network, scored 0.43–0.48 ([PathwayBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16875); [GeoConvention 2025](https://geoconvention.com/wp-content/uploads/abstracts/2025/114857-from-imagery-to-graphs-with-scalable-ai)). TraversRL (ECCV 2026) "more than doubles connectivity metrics" over segmentation, but its code has not been released ([arXiv 2607.17479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17479)). The lesson for Lebanon is to generate the network from OSM centrelines and building frontage and then attach imagery-derived geometry to it. AccessMap's sidewalkify (MIT/Apache-2.0) offsets annotated street centrelines and repairs overshoots ([sidewalkify](https://github.com/AccessMap/sidewalkify)), and crossify completes the crossings ([crossify](https://github.com/AccessMap/crossify)). An OpenSidewalks maintainer warns that centreline offsets alone give only rough results ([OSM accessibility list](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/accessibility/2021-January/000446.html)), which is why the offsets must then be clipped against footprints and traced curbs.

For width from polygons, the established recipe is a skeleton plus a distance transform, with width at each skeleton point equal to twice the distance to the boundary. Hosseini et al. report width RMSE of **1.85 pixels for 7–14 px sidewalks and 3.12 px for narrower ones** ([Hosseini 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06120)). Meli Harvey's sidewalk-widths code applies the centreline-to-edge method to city polygons and has been reused worldwide ([sidewalkwidths-nyc](https://github.com/meliharvey/sidewalkwidths-nyc/blob/master/README.md)), and the `centerline` package (MIT) and `pygeoops` provide the medial-axis step ([centerline](https://pypi.org/project/centerline); [pygeoops](https://pypi.org/project/pygeoops)). In metres, a 2–3 px error is **±0.6–1.0 m at 0.31 m GSD, ±0.9–1.6 m at 0.50 m and ±0.25–0.4 m on the 12 cm drone ortho**. Typical Beirut sidewalks are 1–2 m wide, so overhead width at satellite resolution supports width *classes*, not measurements.

### Street-level width hinges on one number: camera height

UrbanVGGT segments sidewalk and road with SegFormer, builds a per-pixel point map with VGGT, fits the ground plane with RANSAC, and scales the result with a 2.5 m camera-height prior. On about 300 Washington DC Street View images it reached **MAE 0.252 m, with 95.5% of estimates within 0.5 m**. Without scale calibration the MAE was 1.571 m, and moving the height prior by ±0.5 m pushed it to 0.35–0.38 m with strong bias ([UrbanVGGT](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22531v1)). In the paper's backbone table, models used at native metric scale did badly (DepthPro 0.77 m, Metric3Dv2 1.36 m, Depth Anything V2 1.71 m), with UniDepthV2 the exception at 0.29 m. No code or licence has been released ([UrbanVGGT](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22531v1)).

Project Sidewalk tried the same idea on iPhone photos from Seoul with Mask2Former Mapillary-Vistas segmentation and got a **clear-width MAE of 0.77 m**. Pitch was the dominant error, with ±2° producing ±10–16% width error, and the worst failures came from sidewalks touching the frame edge and poles truncating the span ([RampNet #217](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/RampNet/issues/217)). The same thread gives a geometry budget the Beirut team should adopt. From 2.5 m height, 1° of angle covers about 0.3 m of ground at 6 m but 1.6 m at 15 m, so measurements should be taken at the nearest point; and the plane should be fitted to the sidewalk, not the road, because a 15 cm curb otherwise inflates width by about 6% ([RampNet #217](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/RampNet/issues/217)). Vision-language models are no shortcut. On 514 laser-measured Seoul images the best calibrated width interval was about ±1.0 m, every model overestimated width, and the authors conclude that "response self-consistency is therefore not evidence of accuracy" ([Lieu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17882)).

Mapillary helps with scale. Its API exposes `camera_parameters`, `computed_geometry`, `computed_rotation`, `sfm_cluster` point-cloud URLs and `mesh` URLs for each image ([Mapillary SDK](https://mapillary.github.io/mapillary-python-sdk/docs/mapillary.config.api/mapillary.config.api.entities)). The only open tool built on those poses is street-width-lab. It is simulation-only and unlicensed, but it uses parked-car wheelbases as scale bars, an idea unusually well suited to Beirut ([street-width-lab](https://github.com/kauevestena/street-width-lab)). HeiGIT ran SAM3 plus Depth Anything V2 on about 80,000 Mapillary images of Łódź and published no accuracy figures, saying only that the output "has some anomalies" ([HeiGIT](https://heigit.org/measuring-what-you-never-notice-a-computer-vision-approach-to-accessible-paths/)).

### Curb height and slope are field attributes, and condition is a weak automated signal

No validated method measures curb height from street imagery. Project Sidewalk scopes slope and lip height out of imagery entirely, because an 8.33% running slope over 1.5 m needs 2–3 cm of vertical accuracy ([RampNet #217](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/RampNet/issues/217)). iOSPointMapper, which uses iPhone LiDAR, reports sidewalk-width RMSE of 1.18–1.31 m and object errors "often below 0.5 m" within 5 m, and it lists curb and slope as future work ([iOSPointMapper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22392v1)). FHWA's pocket-LiDAR study found that some smartphone apps come close to terrestrial-scanner references on curb-ramp slopes ([FHWA-HRT-25-014](https://highways.dot.gov/sites/fhwa.dot.gov/files/FHWA-HRT-25-014.pdf)). Surface condition can be automated only coarsely. Project Sidewalk's DINOv2 validators score AUC 0.855 on surface problems, which **drops to 0.710 in unseen deployments** ([sidewalk-validator-ai](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/sidewalk-validator-ai)). On the Seoul data, three of four VLMs returned prediction sets for pavement grade that included all five grades ([Lieu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17882)). No sidewalk-tile damage dataset exists; the available surface-damage datasets are for road asphalt.

### Obstruction detectors transfer poorly, and Lebanese classes do not exist yet

Generic objects are well served. On the 2026 ROD dataset (MIT licence, 25 pedestrian-view classes, partly captured in Tehran), nano YOLO models reach **mAP@50 of about 0.87–0.88**. Motorcycle recall is only 0.676–0.784, though, and motorcycles are among Beirut's most common sidewalk occupiers ([ROD](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00681)). The hard part is context. When Duan et al. removed San Pedro Garza García (Mexico) from training, **obstacle recall fell from 60.8% to 42.0% and missing-curb-ramp recall from 88.4% to 46.6%**, and US models missed stepped sidewalks "because most sidewalk obstacles in US cities are not stairs" ([Duan et al. 2022](https://makeabilitylab.cs.washington.edu/media/publications/Duan_ScalingCrowdAiSidewalkAccessibilityAssessmentsInitialExperimentsExaminingLabelQualityAndCrossCityTrainingOnPerformance_ASSETS222022.pdf)). Geographic bias in street-scene models sits mostly in classification rather than localisation, and unsupervised domain adaptation did no better than source-only training for the USA-to-India shift ([Nair et al. 2024](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2412.11061)).

Open-vocabulary detectors over-fire when used on their own: on curb ramps OWLv2 reached F1 0.184 against 0.855 for a fine-tuned detector ([RampNet #52](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/RampNet/issues/52)). They recover with light tuning, though. Grounding DINO went from 25–27% zero-shot AP to 73–78% after LoRA fine-tuning on roadside infrastructure ([SVII-3D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10535)). The evidence on label quality is mixed. For fine-grained tags, a cleaned 24k set beat a noisy 87k one ([sidewalk-tagger-ai](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/sidewalk-tagger-ai)); for coarse obstacle labels, larger noisy data beat smaller validated data ([Duan et al. 2022](https://makeabilitylab.cs.washington.edu/media/publications/Duan_ScalingCrowdAiSidewalkAccessibilityAssessmentsInitialExperimentsExaminingLabelQualityAndCrossCityTrainingOnPerformance_ASSETS222022.pdf)). The practical precedents for localising a label set are Project Sidewalk's deployments in Chandigarh and Mexico; in Chandigarh the team added "parked car", "carts" and "electric box" tags and replaced the example images with local street views ([Lalwani et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09216v1)).

## The Beirut pilot is a sound schema with no data in it yet

The pilot in `pilot_beirut/` is a QGIS project, `Beirut_sidewalk_pilot.qgs`, covering Mar Mikhael, Gemmayzeh, north Achrafieh and the Karantina edge (lon 35.508–35.530, lat 33.884–33.905, about 2 × 2.3 km). Its survey store, `survey/sidewalk_survey.gpkg`, is in EPSG:32636 (UTM 36N, metres) and holds three layers. The `sidewalks` layer takes one polygon per block face, from building edge to curb, with `sw_id`, `street_name`, `side`, `surface` (tiles, concrete, asphalt, stone, unpaved, mixed), `condition` (good to very poor), `has_curb`, `curb_height_cm`, `curb_ramp`, `continuous`, auto-filled `length_m` and `area_m2`, `source_imagery` and `mapillary_image_id`. The `width_measurements` layer holds transect lines drawn every 20–25 m and at pinch points, with `total_width_m` computed from the geometry, a typed `clear_width_m`, and a `method` of imagery, Mapillary or field. The `obstructions` layer holds points whose `ob_type` takes **21 Lebanon-specific values**: parked car, parked motorcycle, generator, vendor, shop display, café furniture, kiosk, utility pole, street light, tree, sign post, bollard, planter, bin, electrical cabinet, low wires, private steps, barrier or valet sign, construction, damage, and other. Each point also records `permanence`, `clear_width_left_m`, `source` and `observed_date`, and `sw_id` is snapped from the nearest sidewalk within 10 m. When inspected on 6 November 2026, **all three layers held zero features** and both Mapillary GeoJSON files were empty placeholders, because `fetch_mapillary.py` had not yet been run with a token.

The reference layers are in place. Within the pilot extent they hold 1,288 OSM street ways (about 136 km of centreline, 79 km of it residential, tertiary, secondary, primary and unclassified streets), 888 OSM street features, 3,531 OSM and 3,254 Microsoft building footprints, and 178 HeiGIT road lines with surface and width class. At two sides per local street, that implies roughly **150–170 km of potential block-face sidewalk** in a 4.6 km² pilot, a figure computed here from `osm_streets.geojson` that sets the scale of the census. Imagery is limited to tracing backdrops: Esri World Imagery current (2024, 0.46–0.50 m), Esri Wayback releases from 2014 and 2016 (0.31 m WorldView-3), an OSM basemap, and the 12 cm CC-BY drone orthophoto of 4 August 2020, which covers only the port. The README warns mappers that Esri can be offset by 5–8 m, restricts Esri to viewing and manual tracing with no bulk download or AI training, and reserves AI work for the drone ortho and Mapillary. Both scripts use only the Python standard library: `download_base_data.py` accepts any bounding box, and `fetch_mapillary.py` splits the request into 0.005° cells and pulls image points and map features.

Three of the pilot's design choices are right and should become the framework's foundations. The **per-side, block-face `sw_id`** is the correct unit of analysis: it matches the MIT/AUB Community Streets practice of modelling arterials as two parallel sidewalks, the audit-unit logic of MAPS, and an OpenSidewalks edge ([Community Streets report](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/daralhandas9921-dargroupcor87ce-prod-3847/media/Project/dargroup/dargroup/Insights/Report_Community-Streets_Prof-Sevtsuk.pdf)). Separating **total width from clear width** captures something that São Paulo's and Richmond's city polygons cannot ([RampNet #217](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/RampNet/issues/217)). And splitting **obstructions by permanence** maps directly onto Beirut's enforcement instruments.

Four gaps need closing before the pilot can feed models. The polygon width rule (`area_m2 / length_m`) should give way to medial-axis transects. Obstructions need an occupied-width field and a time of observation, because occupation by cars, café tables and vendors changes through the day. Every record needs provenance fields for automated outputs, covering method, model version, confidence and validator. And the licence note needs correcting: the README tells users to "share derived data alike" under CC BY-SA, but Mapillary's own terms say that derived data published outside OSM must be **ODbL** ([Mapillary Help](https://help.mapillary.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001777705)), so the publication licence should be ODbL from the start.

## The target framework hangs eight modules on one segment ID

The architecture inverts the US template. In US cities, municipal planimetrics train an aerial model and street view fills the gaps. Lebanon has no such planimetrics: in November 2026 OSM held **32 separately mapped sidewalks and zero sidewalk widths nationwide** ([Overpass API](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)). The network therefore has to be generated, the measurements have to come from the street, and overhead imagery becomes an optional prior. Every module reads and writes against the pilot's `sw_id` segment table, and every attribute carries the method that produced it.

### Module 1: licences decide which imagery may touch a model

The licence boundary in the pilot README is the right one and should be enforced in code. Esri tiles stay inside QGIS for human tracing and never enter a training or inference pipeline. Machine-learning steps read only the CC-BY port ortho, Mapillary images, the team's own capture, and commercial imagery bought with derived-product rights confirmed in writing; archive 30 cm imagery costs about $19/km² with a 1 km² minimum ([SkyWatch](https://skywatch.com/data)). The Maxar 2020 OAM scenes are CC BY-NC. They are acceptable for non-commercial research, but their outputs inherit a non-commercial constraint that conflicts with an ODbL release, so they are best kept out of anything published.

Two Beirut vector assets are worth more than any imagery purchase, and both must be requested. The AUB 2010 curbline layer, measured against the Microsoft or Beirut Urban Lab footprints, gives a first-pass built width for all of Municipal Beirut. The MIT/AUB Community Streets per-street table is the only prior estimate of presence, width and obstruction. It found **6% of streets without sidewalks, about 17% of sidewalks permanently obstructed, and about 20% narrower than 1.2 m** ([Community Streets report](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/daralhandas9921-dargroupcor87ce-prod-3847/media/Project/dargroup/dargroup/Insights/Report_Community-Streets_Prof-Sevtsuk.pdf)).

The street-level backbone should be the team's own walking capture rather than archive Mapillary. Walking puts the camera on the pedestrian side of parked cars and fixes the camera height that the width module depends on. Mapillary recommends 24–25 fps 360° video at 5.6K–8K ([Mapillary Help](https://help.mapillary.com/hc/en-us/articles/360012674619)). `fetch_mapillary.py` should gain the `camera_parameters`, `computed_rotation`, `computed_altitude`, `sfm_cluster` and `mesh` fields ([Mapillary SDK](https://mapillary.github.io/mapillary-python-sdk/docs/mapillary.config.api/mapillary.config.api.entities)), plus an image-download step, so that archive imagery can be measured and mined as well as viewed. Drones remain off the table, since the Army suspended all permits on 1 March 2026 ([LBCI](https://v7cdn.lbcgroup.tv/news/lebanon-news/908645/lebanese-army-suspends-drone-permits-nationwide-amid-regional-tensions/en)).

### Module 2: generate the network first and segment overhead imagery second

The segment spine comes from running sidewalkify on OSM centrelines, with offsets taken from HeiGIT carriageway width or a street-class default. The offsets are then clipped to Microsoft footprint frontage, which in old Beirut usually sits on the lot line, split into per-side block faces carrying the pilot's `sw_id`, and joined by crossings seeded with crossify. Polygons come from three sources in descending order of trust. The first is manual tracing in QGIS over the sharpest Esri or Wayback date, registered to the footprints to remove the 5–8 m offset. The second is the AUB curblines, wherever they still match post-2020 conditions. The third is SAMGeo point-prompted masks on the 12 cm port ortho and on any licensed imagery. Width then comes from medial-axis transects, using Tile2Net's polygon-to-network code (BSD-3) or `centerline`, sampled every 20–25 m to match the pilot protocol and reported as median and minimum width per segment.

Automatic overhead extraction belongs late in the plan, and only if licensed 30 cm imagery is bought. Its role then is screening for sidewalk presence in cities outside Beirut, not measurement. Fine-tuning would start from Tile2Net weights, but because no training module exists the team would have to recover the HMSA training code from NVIDIA's segmentation codebase, or train a SegFormer or UNet on local masks instead. GeoSAM's 560-image training set and single RTX 4090 suggest the order of magnitude: about **2–5 km² of carefully labelled tiles on one 24 GB GPU** ([GeoSAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.11319v2)). That is an inference, not a measured learning curve. The pilot's traced polygons become the labels, but only after they are re-registered onto the licensed imagery, since they were drawn over Esri.

### Module 3: street-level measurement anchored by a known camera height

The measurement module reimplements the UrbanVGGT recipe with Beirut-specific safeguards. Each image, or a 90° perspective crop rectified from the 360° frame to face the sidewalk, goes through the Mask2Former Mapillary-Vistas model (`facebook/mask2former-swin-large-mapillary-vistas-semantic`), whose sidewalk, curb and curb-cut classes Cityscapes lacks ([RampNet #217](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/RampNet/issues/217); [Vistas v2](https://blog.mapillary.com/update/2021/01/18/vistas-2-dataset.html)). VGGT or MoGe then produces a point map. The module fits the *sidewalk* plane with RANSAC, scales it by the recorded rig height, and measures the curb-to-facade distance only within the nearest 3–8 m. It rejects frames in which the sidewalk touches the image edge or a pole truncates the span, the two failure modes behind Project Sidewalk's largest errors ([RampNet #217](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/RampNet/issues/217)), and takes the median per segment, as UrbanVGGT does with samples about every 30 m. For archive Mapillary sequences with unknown camera heights, the fallback is MapAnything posed with Mapillary's computed poses, the route Project Sidewalk has planned, with scale checked against parked-car wheelbases as street-width-lab proposes.

Checkpoint licences matter if the outputs are to be released under ODbL and reused by government. VGGT's code allows commercial use, but its commercial checkpoint is released on application and the original VGGT-1B is non-commercial ([VGGT](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vggt)). MoGe's code is MIT, but its weight licence is not stated ([MoGe](https://github.com/microsoft/MoGe)). UniDepthV2, the best native-scale model, is **CC BY-NC 4.0**, and Depth Anything V2 is commercially usable only in its Small size ([Ultralytics overview](https://www.ultralytics.com/blog/best-depth-estimation-models)). MASt3R is CC BY-NC-SA ([MASt3R](https://github.com/naver/mast3r)). Even the Vistas licence is reported inconsistently: the original paper says CC-BY-SA ([Neuhold et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2017/papers/Neuhold_The_Mapillary_Vistas_ICCV_2017_paper.pdf)) while Dataset Ninja lists CC BY-NC-SA ([Dataset Ninja](https://datasetninja.com/mapillary-vistas-dataset)). That question has to be settled with Mapillary before a Vistas-trained model goes into production.

Curb attributes split in two. From imagery, the module should output only a categorical curb class (raised, flush or absent), derived from Vistas curb masks plus the offset between the road and sidewalk planes; that use is plausible but unvalidated. Curb height in centimetres, ramp slope and cross slope come from the field sample, measured with tape and level or with phone LiDAR at under 5 m, and fill the pilot's existing `curb_height_cm` field.

### Module 4: obstruction detection mapped to the pilot's 21 types

The detector stack works in layers. A Vistas segmentation mask defines where the sidewalk is. A YOLO-class or RT-DETR detector, fine-tuned on ROD plus Beirut labels, finds the objects. Mask-box overlap logic then decides whether each object stands *on* the sidewalk and how much clear width it removes. Finally, multi-view triangulation places each object before it is deduplicated and snapped to `sw_id`; Mapillary itself requires a detection in at least three images before it creates a point feature ([Mapillary Help](https://help.mapillary.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002332165)). Ultralytics distributes its YOLO models under AGPL-3.0 with a paid enterprise option. This research did not verify that, so it should be checked before the detector is embedded in any service, and RT-DETR implementations are an alternative. Open-vocabulary detectors and SAM 3 text prompts ("diesel generator", "street vendor cart") are used only to *mine candidates* for human verification, never as the final detector.

|---|---|---|
| parked_car | ROD Car (recall 0.79–0.84) | Low; the overlap logic with the sidewalk mask is the real work |
| parked_motorcycle | ROD Motorcycle (recall 0.68–0.78) | High; the weakest Lebanon-relevant class ([ROD](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00681)) |
| generator | None | High; new class |

About half the classes start from usable pretrained detectors. The commercial-occupation classes that matter most for enforcement (generators, vendors, shop goods, kiosks and construction) have no prior data and need a Lebanese labelling campaign. Project Sidewalk is the proven human-in-the-loop tool for that campaign. Its code is MIT, its data is CC0, it accepts `pano_source=mapillary`, and new cities are onboarded by emailing the team ([Project Sidewalk API](https://sidewalk-dc.cs.washington.edu/api); [SidewalkWebpage](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/SidewalkWebpage)). A Beirut deployment would carry the pilot's 21 types as Obstacle and Surface Problem tags with Beirut tooltip images, and it should retrain the DINOv2 validator locally, since the validator performs worst in its non-US deployment ([sidewalk-validator-ai](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/sidewalk-validator-ai)). Global Streetscapes, about 10 million Mapillary and KartaView images under CC-BY-SA-4.0, supplies pretraining scenes from comparable cities ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NUS-UAL/global-streetscapes)). No public study measures how many labels a new class needs. The best available guide is the Mexican evidence of thousands of crops per class with pooled multi-city training ([Duan et al. 2022](https://makeabilitylab.cs.washington.edu/media/publications/Duan_ScalingCrowdAiSidewalkAccessibilityAssessmentsInitialExperimentsExaminingLabelQualityAndCrossCityTrainingOnPerformance_ASSETS222022.pdf)).

### Module 5: fuse by method rank, not by averaging

Fusion keeps every observation in a long table, recording segment, attribute, value, method, date, model version and confidence. A derived segment table then picks each value by an explicit hierarchy: field measurement first, then validated automated measurement from the team's own fixed-height capture, then manual tracing on imagery, then automated measurement from archive Mapillary, then automated overhead output, and finally priors such as curbline-to-footprint distance or HeiGIT carriageway class. The error tables from Module 6 re-weight that hierarchy. The design follows Ning et al., where aerial extraction provides coverage and street view restores occlusions ([Ning et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2399808321995817)). A Zurich comparison found that spatial-data methods gave broader coverage and more accurate widths than street view alone ([Zenodo 15182508](https://zenodo.org/records/15182508)), which argues for fusion rather than for either source alone. Total width is fused geometrically, with the inner edge from the registered footprint and the outer edge from the best curb evidence. Clear width is total width minus the occupied width of permanent obstructions at each transect. Temporary occupation sits in a separate, dated layer, so a single Tuesday-morning snapshot is never mistaken for the street's built condition.

### Module 6: QA gates automation on local error

Accuracy has to be measured in Beirut, because no published method has been validated there. Each pilot phase should field-measure a stratified random sample of about 10% of segments with tape or laser, stratified by street class, frontage type and district. A further 5% should be re-measured by RTK through LEB-CORS or with phone LiDAR, and 10% double-audited. Attribute labels need at least three independent Beirut validators, because Project Sidewalk found that five labellers per street lift recall from 68% to 92% while precision holds near 70% ([Saha et al. 2019](https://makeabilitylab.cs.washington.edu/media/publications/Saha_ProjectSidewalkAWebBasedCrowdsourcingToolForCollectingSidewalkAccessibilityDataAtScale_CHI2019.pdf)). Width code should first be benchmarked on the Seoul CC0 dataset of 514 laser-measured images on Zenodo ([Lieu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17882)), so that software bugs are separated from domain shift. The metrics to report are completeness and correctness by length within a matching buffer (Tile2Net used 4 m), polygon IoU, width MAE and the share within ±0.5 m, Cohen's kappa for categorical attributes, and PathwayBench-style traversability for the network ([PathwayBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16875)). An automated module goes live only after it meets an acceptance threshold on the Beirut sample, and every release passes `python-osw-validation` before publication ([OSW](https://github.com/OpenSidewalks/OpenSidewalks-Schema)).

### Module 7: indicators a municipality can enforce

Indicators should be simple, measurable from imagery, and tied to instruments that already exist. The core set extends the Community Streets baselines with what that work could not capture: sidewalk presence as a share of street length; effective clear width, with the shares below 1.2 m, 1.5 m and 2.0 m; permanent obstructions per 100 m; temporary occupation per 100 m by type and time of day; curb and continuity (flush, missing, or steps into buildings); and Project Sidewalk-style 1–5 severity, on which Azcapotzalco averaged 4.1 for obstacles against 2.2–3.2 in US cities ([Froehlich et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.13626)). Obstruction categories should be coded against **Beirut Governor's Warning No. 11866/2026**, which targets tables, chairs, planters, barriers, goods displays, stalls, sidewalk parking and valet operations, with inspections from **16 November 2026** ([Libnanews](https://en.libnanews.com/beirut-abboud-sets-violators-free-sidewalks-global-voices)). Coding to it turns the dataset into an evaluation of enforcement.

Any composite score should weight blockage above nominal width, because around AUB sidewalk blockage shaped pedestrian satisfaction and width did not ([Said & Abou-Zeid 2015](https://www.aub.edu.lb/Neighborhood/Documents/mahersaid-mayaabouzeid2015.pdf)). Segment widths should also feed back into the Community Streets pedestrian-flow model. Its PM calibration explained only 46% of variance, which the authors attributed partly to missing official built-environment data ([Community Streets report](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/daralhandas9921-dargroupcor87ce-prod-3847/media/Project/dargroup/dargroup/Insights/Report_Community-Streets_Prof-Sevtsuk.pdf)). Achrafieh's hills make the TDEI DEM-based incline job a high-value addition.

### Module 8: publish in OpenSidewalks and contribute to OSM by consent

The internal exchange format should be OpenSidewalks v0.3 GeoJSON, a network schema built directly on OSM tags (`highway=footway` with `footway=sidewalk`, `barrier=kerb` with `kerb=raised|lowered|flush`, and numeric `width`, `incline` and `surface`), with custom entities for Lebanese extensions ([OSW](https://github.com/OpenSidewalks/OpenSidewalks-Schema)). The pilot maps onto it cleanly. The `sidewalks` polygons derive OSW edges that carry `width`, `has_curb` becomes kerb nodes at crossings, `surface=tiles` maps to `paving_stones`, and `obstructions` become adjacent points with extension tags for type, permanence and occupied width. The open release should be GeoPackage plus OSW GeoJSON under **ODbL**. Mapillary's terms for derived data published outside OSM require that licence, and it is compatible with OSM and the Microsoft footprints ([Mapillary Help](https://help.mapillary.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001777705)). Blurred imagery goes back to Mapillary or a self-hosted Panoramax. Contributing to OSM is a separate and slower channel. Any bulk upload counts as an import and needs local community buy-in and an import-list review ([OSM Import Guidelines](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/Import/Guidelines)). The choice between separate sidewalk ways and `sidewalk:*` tags on the street should be agreed with Lebanese mappers first ([OSM Sidewalks](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Sidewalks)), and generated candidates should then be validated one by one in a Tasking Manager or MapRoulette workflow.

## Expect half-metre widths and publish the error bars

The table below translates published benchmarks into Beirut expectations. The right-hand column is a judgement from the evidence, not a measured result, and it is the hypothesis the pilot's ground truth should test.

| Attribute | Best published result (context) | Realistic Beirut expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Sidewalk presence per segment | Crowd "no sidewalk" labels 69% P / 79% R with one label, 80% P / 67% R with two or more ([Project Sidewalk API](https://sidewalk-amsterdam.cs.washington.edu/v3/api-docs/sidewalkPresence)) | Above 90% agreement with three or more local validators and own imagery; lower in the curb-less old quarters |

| Surface condition | Validator AUC 0.855, 0.710 unseen ([sidewalk-validator-ai](https://github.com/ProjectSidewalk/sidewalk-validator-ai)) | Coarse three-level human rating; automation for screening only |
| Generic obstructions (cars, poles, trees, bins) | mAP@50 about 0.88 on ROD ([ROD](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00681)) | Recall of 0.8 or more after local fine-tuning; the "on the sidewalk" logic is the main error source |

| Network routability | Traversability 0.04–0.35 for Tile2Net, 0.43–0.48 for Prophet ([PathwayBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16875)) | High if generated from the OSM graph plus crossings and then corrected |

Two points stand out. The 0.25 m headline does not transfer automatically: it came from one GSV benchmark with a known vehicle height, Project Sidewalk's heterogeneous phone photos tripled the error, and UrbanVGGT's own authors caution against "broad claims of cross-domain robustness" ([UrbanVGGT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22531)). And the dominant Beirut failure modes, namely parked-car occlusion, very narrow sidewalks, and non-planar ramps and steps, are exactly the conditions on which the street-level methods were *not* validated. The framework responds by measuring at short range from the pedestrian side, keeping field ground truth on a fixed share of segments in every phase, and publishing per-attribute uncertainty alongside every value.

## Build order: ground truth first, models second, overhead last

The sequence follows one rule: nothing automated ships until the pilot's hand-measured segments can score it. The pilot area becomes the calibration lab, Municipal Beirut the first scale-up, and the other cities a stratified sample.

Phase 0 is the only time-critical step. Today is 6 November 2026, which leaves **ten days** to capture the pre-enforcement state of the Gemmayzeh and Mar Mikhael frontages; after the 16th that state cannot be recreated. Phases 2 and 3 can run in parallel once Phase 1 has produced labelled imagery. Phase 5 comes last, deliberately, and is optional: the evidence says it buys sidewalk-presence screening for cities without street imagery, not measurements, and it is the one module whose imagery must be paid for.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "automating" a sidewalk inventory means for Lebanon. Automation here is a calibration problem, not a model that replaces mappers. Every published method carries a hidden dependency that Lebanon lacks: municipal planimetrics for Tile2Net, a known camera height for UrbanVGGT, and US obstacle distributions for Project Sidewalk's detectors. The framework succeeds or fails on whether it manufactures those dependencies locally through hand-measured segments, fixed-height capture and Lebanese obstruction labels. The Beirut pilot's empty GeoPackage is already the right container for all three, and its first complete census is worth more than any model.

The second implication concerns policy rather than technology. Lebanon's distinctive sidewalk problems (cars, generators, vendors and café furniture) are temporal and commercial, and Beirut now has a legal typology and an enforcement date for exactly those problems. A framework that dates every observation, separates permanent from temporary occupation, and codes both to Warning 11866/2026 becomes a repeatable monitoring instrument rather than a one-off map. Repeat monitoring is what South Asian cities have found they need, as hawkers in Pune return within days of clearance drives ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/pune/footpaths-in-pune-blocked-by-hawkers-and-parked-vehicles-causing-major-hassles-for-pedestrians)). The open questions can all be settled within the first phases rather than blocking the plan: whether the width method stays under 0.5 m of error on Beirut's narrow, car-lined sidewalks, how many labels a "generator" class needs, and whether the Vistas and VGGT licences permit an ODbL government release.
